Ingredient substitutions

By Crystal Allen and Ken Allen · Updated [DATE]

Missing something? Here's what to use instead. Some swaps are one-for-one; others are families of ingredients you can trade freely. Either way, think about flavor, texture and cooking time, and taste as you go.

Live cultures and heat. Heat can kill the live cultures in fermented foods like yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut and kimchi. To keep them, stir these in after cooking. (Harvard Health)

Fermented foods

Fermented foods photo

Think about why the recipe uses the ferment. If it's for tang and depth, almost any fermented food in the same group will do. If it's for live cultures, use it uncooked or stir it in at the end.

Substitutions: Fermented foods
IngredientAmountUse insteadNotesSource
Buttermilk (with kefir) 1 cup
  • 1 cup plain unsweetened kefir
 Our suggestion: kefir is tangy and pourable like buttermilk. Heat can kill the live cultures in fermented foods, so in cooked or baked dishes this swap is about flavor and texture. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Note source
Sauerkraut ½ cup
  • ½ cup kimchi (spicier, with garlic and chili)
 Our suggestion. To keep live cultures, add either one after cooking. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Note source
Tangy cultured dairy — Interchangeable
  • Plain yogurt
  • Greek yogurt thinned with a little water or milk
  • Kefir
  • Buttermilk
 Interchangeable in dressings, marinades and overnight oats. For live cultures, use them uncooked. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Fermented vegetables — Interchangeable
  • Sauerkraut
  • Kimchi
  • Naturally fermented pickles (from the refrigerated case)
 Swap freely as a topping or side; kimchi brings more heat and garlic. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Fermented pastes and sauces — Interchangeable
  • White or yellow miso
  • Red miso
  • Doenjang
  • Gochujang
 All add salty, savory depth. Lighter miso is milder; red miso and doenjang are stronger; gochujang is spicy-sweet, so start with less. Our suggestion · not yet tested

Whole grains

Whole grains photo

Most whole grains can stand in for each other once cooked. The difference is time and texture, so cook the substitute on its own before adding it to the dish.

Substitutions: Whole grains
IngredientAmountUse insteadNotesSource
Brown rice (in bowls and fried rice) 3 cups cooked
  • 3 cups cooked farro or barley
 Our suggestion. Cook the grain separately first; cooking times differ. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Chia seeds (in overnight oats) 2 tbsp
  • 2 tbsp ground flaxseed
 Our suggestion. Flax won't gel as firmly as chia. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Chewy grains for bowls and salads — Interchangeable
  • Farro
  • Barley
  • Wheat berries
  • Brown rice
 Cook each separately first; cooking times differ. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Quick-cooking grains — Interchangeable
  • Bulgur
  • Quinoa
  • Millet
 Good swaps for each other in pilafs and stuffings; check the package for water and time. Our suggestion · not yet tested

Beans and lentils

Beans and lentils photo

Ask whether the recipe needs beans that hold their shape or beans that melt into a sauce, and swap within that group.

Substitutions: Beans and lentils
IngredientAmountUse insteadNotesSource
Hold their shape (salads, bowls) — Interchangeable
  • Green or French lentils
  • Black beans
  • Chickpeas
  • White beans
 Use when you want distinct, firm pieces. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Cook down creamy (dals, soups) — Interchangeable
  • Red lentils
  • Yellow lentils
  • Split peas
 These break down as they cook; don't swap them into salads. Our suggestion · not yet tested

Greens

Greens photo

Swap greens by sturdiness. Tender greens wilt in seconds; sturdy greens need longer cooking.

Substitutions: Greens
IngredientAmountUse insteadNotesSource
Mild and tender — Interchangeable
  • Baby spinach
  • Swiss chard
  • Butter lettuce
 Add at the end of cooking or eat raw. Our suggestion · not yet tested
Sturdy — Interchangeable
  • Kale
  • Collard greens
  • Cabbage
 Need longer cooking, or massage kale with a little oil and salt for salads. Our suggestion · not yet tested
