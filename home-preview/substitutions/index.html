Ingredient substitutions

By Crystal Allen and Ken Allen · Updated [DATE]

Missing something? Here's what to use instead. Some swaps are one-for-one; others are families of ingredients you can trade freely. Either way, think about flavor, texture and cooking time, and taste as you go.

Live cultures and heat. Heat can kill the live cultures in fermented foods like yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut and kimchi. To keep them, stir these in after cooking. (Harvard Health)

Fermented foods

Fermented foods photo

Think about why the recipe uses the ferment. If it's for tang and depth, almost any fermented food in the same group will do. If it's for live cultures, use it uncooked or stir it in at the end.

Buttermilk (with kefir)

1 cup

Use instead

  • 1 cup plain unsweetened kefir

Our suggestion: kefir is tangy and pourable like buttermilk. Heat can kill the live cultures in fermented foods, so in cooked or baked dishes this swap is about flavor and texture.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Source: Harvard Health: Do fermented foods live up to the hype?

Sauerkraut

½ cup

Use instead

  • ½ cup kimchi (spicier, with garlic and chili)

Our suggestion. To keep live cultures, add either one after cooking.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Source: Harvard Health: Do fermented foods live up to the hype?

Tangy cultured dairy

Interchangeable

  • Plain yogurt
  • Greek yogurt thinned with a little water or milk
  • Kefir
  • Buttermilk

Interchangeable in dressings, marinades and overnight oats. For live cultures, use them uncooked.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Fermented vegetables

Interchangeable

  • Sauerkraut
  • Kimchi
  • Naturally fermented pickles (from the refrigerated case)

Swap freely as a topping or side; kimchi brings more heat and garlic.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Fermented pastes and sauces

Interchangeable

  • White or yellow miso
  • Red miso
  • Doenjang
  • Gochujang

All add salty, savory depth. Lighter miso is milder; red miso and doenjang are stronger; gochujang is spicy-sweet, so start with less.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Dairy

Dairy photo

Match texture first. Thin, pourable dairy swaps with other pourable dairy; thick, spoonable dairy swaps with other thick dairy.

Buttermilk

1 cup

Use instead

  • 1 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 cup milk minus 1 tbsp, plus 1 tbsp vinegar or lemon juice (let stand 5 minutes)

Heat can kill the live cultures in fermented foods, so in cooked or baked dishes this swap is about flavor and texture.

Whole grains

Whole grains photo

Most whole grains can stand in for each other once cooked. The difference is time and texture, so cook the substitute on its own before adding it to the dish.

Brown rice (in bowls and fried rice)

3 cups cooked

Use instead

  • 3 cups cooked farro or barley

Our suggestion. Cook the grain separately first; cooking times differ.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Chia seeds (in overnight oats)

2 tbsp

Use instead

  • 2 tbsp ground flaxseed

Our suggestion. Flax won't gel as firmly as chia.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Chewy grains for bowls and salads

Interchangeable

  • Farro
  • Barley
  • Wheat berries
  • Brown rice

Cook each separately first; cooking times differ.

Quick-cooking grains

Interchangeable

  • Bulgur
  • Quinoa
  • Millet

Good swaps for each other in pilafs and stuffings; check the package for water and time.

Beans and lentils

Beans and lentils photo

Ask whether the recipe needs beans that hold their shape or beans that melt into a sauce, and swap within that group.

Hold their shape (salads, bowls)

Interchangeable

  • Green or French lentils
  • Black beans
  • Chickpeas
  • White beans

Use when you want distinct, firm pieces.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Cook down creamy (dals, soups)

Interchangeable

  • Red lentils
  • Yellow lentils
  • Split peas

These break down as they cook; don't swap them into salads.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Greens

Greens photo

Swap greens by sturdiness. Tender greens wilt in seconds; sturdy greens need longer cooking.

Mild and tender

Interchangeable

  • Baby spinach
  • Swiss chard
  • Butter lettuce

Add at the end of cooking or eat raw.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Sturdy

Interchangeable

  • Kale
  • Collard greens
  • Cabbage

Need longer cooking, or massage kale with a little oil and salt for salads.

Our suggestion · not yet tested

Baking

Baking photo

Baking is chemistry: swaps here are measured, so follow the amounts closely.

Baking powder

1 tsp

Use instead

  • ¼ tsp baking soda plus ⅝ tsp cream of tartar
  • ¼ tsp baking soda plus ½ cup buttermilk, sour milk or yogurt (reduce the recipe's liquid by ½ cup)
