Meet your gut microbiome

Diet can change gut microbes within about a day. Most findings so far come from small or short studies, so we treat them as early signals, not proof.

In our recipes: Why we focus on everyday eating patterns instead of quick fixes.

Sources: Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health: The Microbiome, NIH News in Health: Your Body's Bugs, David et al., Nature 2014

Fiber: the strongest evidence

Draft

Fiber: the strongest evidence

Higher fiber intake is linked to lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer. Fiber alone hasn't been shown to raise microbial diversity in the short term.

In our recipes: Why most of our recipes are built on beans, lentils, whole grains and vegetables.

Sources: Reynolds et al., The Lancet 2019, King's College London, 2018, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health: Fiber

Fermented foods: promising, early

Mockup

Fermented foods: promising, early

An early Stanford study found a fermented-food diet raised gut microbial diversity. Experts say the health benefits aren't yet established.

In our recipes: Why kefir, kimchi and sauerkraut show up often, and why we add kimchi at the end of cooking to keep its live cultures.

Sources: Wastyk et al., Cell 2021, Marco et al., Nature Reviews Gastroenterology & Hepatology 2021 (ISAPP), American Gastroenterological Association: The gut microbiome

Plant variety and "30 plants a week"

Mockup

Plant variety and "30 plants a week"

Researchers are testing whether eating 30 or more plant types a week helps the gut. For now it's an association, not a proven cause.

In our recipes: Why our recipes mix several vegetables, grains, nuts, seeds and herbs in one dish.

Sources: McDonald et al., mSystems 2018, King's College London, 2021

Patterns beat single foods

Mockup

Patterns beat single foods

Mediterranean-style eating has been linked to gut-microbe changes in older adults, and the benefits faded when people stopped.

In our recipes: Why we offer a full week of meals rather than a single "gut-health" food.

Sources: Ghosh et al., Gut 2020, Ross et al., Nature Reviews Microbiology 2024, MD Anderson: beans and gut health trial

Start slow, stay safe

Mockup

Start slow, stay safe

Add fiber gradually and drink water with it. People with IBS, IBD or strictures, those taking MAOIs, anyone watching sodium, and people who are immunocompromised should take extra care with high-fiber and fermented foods.

In our recipes: Why our recipes carry "Good to know" notes and suggest half portions for beginners.

Sources: Ascension, 2026, UF/IFAS: Sauerkraut, Mayo Clinic: Probiotics
